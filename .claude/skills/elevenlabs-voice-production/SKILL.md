# ElevenLabs Voice Production

Produce narration as independently recoverable sentence clips while preserving
provider originals, measured timing, and clean transitions.

## Core workflow

1. Locate the script, voice assignments, generation code, cached provider
   responses, cleaned clips, combined narration, and rendered video.
2. Keep API keys in the approved keychain or environment. Never print, commit,
   paste, or copy a key into a command that exposes it in logs.
3. Request timestamped output and save the raw provider audio plus alignment as
   an immutable cache. Key the cache by text, voice ID, model, settings, and
   seed—not by local cleanup choices.
4. Decode every raw clip to one consistent PCM format before trimming, fading,
   or joining it. Rebuild cleaned clips from the raw cache each time so
   processing never compounds.
5. Derive the spoken boundary from the first and last lexical characters, not
   terminal punctuation alone.
6. Apply reviewed cleanup:
   - Remove a leading artifact only when waveform/listening evidence identifies
     it. Shift every character, word, caption, beat, and sentence timestamp by
     the exact decoded-frame duration removed.
   - Retain a short natural tail after the final lexical character.
   - Fade whatever tail actually exists; never skip the fade merely because
     the provider returned less tail than requested.
   - Append guaranteed digital silence inside the sentence clip before adding
     the editorial hold between sentences.
7. Concatenate decoded PCM and encode the combined narration once. Do not join
   independently encoded MP3 byte streams.
8. Rebuild the timeline, captions, combined narration, and final video.
9. Verify raw clips, cleaned clips, the combined transition, and the rendered
   video as separate artifacts. A clean sentence file does not prove a clean
   joined track.

## Diagnose a suspicious transition

Read [references/traps.md](references/traps.md) before changing audio. Use
`scripts/inspect_audio_boundary.py` on the two raw provider clips and then on
the cleaned pair:

```bash
python3 scripts/inspect_audio_boundary.py \
  --left sentence-01.mp3 \
  --right sentence-02.mp3 \
  --ffmpeg /path/to/ffmpeg
```

Then inspect the same interval in the combined track with `silencedetect`,
`showwavespic`, and `showspectrumpic`. Determine whether the artifact is:

- the left provider tail;
- the right provider head;
- local trimming/fading;
- the silent hold;
- MP3 encoding or joining; or
- a rendered-video muxing problem.

Prefer a local repair when the performance is otherwise approved. Regenerate
only the affected provider sentence when the raw performance itself cannot be
repaired safely.

## Starting cleanup values

Treat these as tested starting points, not universal constants:

- retain up to 200 ms after the last lexical character;
- fade the final 60 ms, clamped to the available tail;
- append 180 ms of exact silence;
- keep the separate editorial hold controlled by the script;
- for a reviewed head click followed by silence, trim through the artifact but
  leave a short natural lead-in before speech.

Compare short, balanced, and full-tail fades on the worst clip before changing
a portfolio-wide default. Do not attenuate a final consonant merely to satisfy
a waveform rule.

## Completion gate

- Decode every deliverable with FFmpeg using error-only logging.
- Confirm expected sample rate, channels, duration, and timeline duration.
- Run the project’s alignment and cleanup regression tests.
- Scan all cleaned tails; no file may end with an audible nonzero waveform.
- Inspect each changed transition visually and by listening.
- Confirm captions and visual beats still align after any head trim.
- Render and decode at least one final video containing the changed transition.
- Report separately what was regenerated by ElevenLabs, what was locally
  rebuilt, and what was verified in the final render.
